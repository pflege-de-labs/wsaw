What changed across every target

Ten days of wsaw scans, re-diffed end to end: 22,746 changes that would have been reported, of which 61 are critical — every one of them a third party reached in reject mode. The rest is dominated by one thing: third-party tracking URLs that are never the same twice.

The findings behind the counts

Four things in this window are worth acting on. Two are about the sites; two are about the watcher.

critical

An OpenAI pixel shipped to four properties at once — and fires after reject

2026-09-08 12:28–13:12Z

Within 45 minutes, bzrcdn.openai.com/sdk/oaiq.min.js and its event endpoint bzr.openai.com/v1/sdk/events appeared on pflege.de, service.pflege.de, curabox.de and PGR, all carrying the same pixel id NCuKoZ3wbS2WqQ — one tag rollout across the group, not four site changes. It accounts for 220 reported changes, and it is not gated by consent: 64 of those are pre-interaction and 63 are in reject mode, where it also sets __cf_bm and _cfuvid cookies on both OpenAI hosts.

critical

Kneipp.Home ignores rejection the most broadly

throughout the window

Kneipp.Home produces 23 of the 61 criticals off only 829 changes — the worst ratio of any target. In reject mode it contacts bing.com, doubleclick.net, facebook.com, facebook.net, google.de, googletagmanager.com, unpkg.com, webgains.io and a Cloud Run host, and sets .bing.com and .doubleclick.net cookies. pflege.de is second at 20 criticals, mostly google.com, google.de, googleadservices.com, doubleclick.net and consentmanager contacted either before any interaction or after rejection.

watcher defect

A reused browser leaked a pflege.de cookie into two unrelated scans

2026-09-11 02:30Z and 03:39Z

An FPID cookie scoped to .pflege.de was recorded as a third-party cookie in a hartmann.info scan and a Kneipp.Home scan — both on browsers marked browserReused, and the hartmann.info scan contacted only hartmann.info, cookiebot.eu and cloudflareinsights.com, so nothing in it could have set that cookie. This is cookie-jar carry-over between scans sharing a browser, and it lands at critical severity: two of the 61 criticals are false positives from the watcher itself.

watcher defect

The GTM container-version rule only fires a third of the time

throughout the window

gtm.js produced 172 script-changed reports, but only 15 of them named a container version — the other 157 compared raw body digests, which is exactly what the bodyIdentity rule exists to prevent. Across the store, only 440 of 1,326 captured gtm.js responses carry an extracted identity at all. Until that extraction works on every capture, GTM is the single largest source of script-change noise (366 of 711).

Why the volume is what it is

22,746 reported changes over ten days is roughly 2,370 a day, and nobody reads that. The composition explains where it comes from — and that most of it is one identifiable problem.

  • 84% of asset-added changes are a URL seen exactly once. 18,950 additions span 16,582 distinct URLs; only 149 URLs recur five times or more. These are per-request tracking URLs whose parameters are not yet normalised away, not new assets.
  • Four domains produce half of all additions: google.com (3,827), consentmanager.net (3,740), doubleclick.net (1,588) and bing.com (1,209). consentmanager.net alone is the single largest source of reported changes of any domain (4,134 across all types).
  • Flap suppression already absorbs 43.7% of raw changes, and allow lists another 12.5%. The remainder is what reaches a reader — so tightening dropQueryParams for the consentmanager delivery/info and Taboola unip shapes, and for the Facebook /tr cas parameter, is the highest-leverage change available.
  • Capture quality is a real contributor. 152 scans were reported degraded — 77 on pflege.de alone — and 30 comparisons were incomparable, nearly all from bursts of ERR_NAME_NOT_RESOLVED that hit every target at once, so a resolver hiccup on the scanning host rather than the sites.
  • Consent interaction is not fully stable. 82 consent-changed reports, dominated by pflege.de flipping applied ↔ failed 13 times in each direction. Each flip also distorts the asset diff on both sides of it.

How this was produced

Changes are derived, so the history was replayed rather than read: every stored result, grouped into (target, consent mode) series, compared with the previous usable result using the same diff.Compare the scanner runs, with each target's allow list, deny list and severity overrides from wsaw.yaml, and a 6-hour flap window applied in scan order across all targets. Errored and skipped scans are never used as a baseline, matching the store's own PreviousResult rule.

  • Source: a read-only snapshot of the live store, 1,447 results, taken 2026-09-11 09:01 local.
  • Tool: go run ./tools/changeanalysis -db snap.db -config wsaw.yaml -csv changes.csv.
  • Caveat: this replays previous-scan baselines, which is the configured detection mode; an approved-baseline comparison would produce different counts.
  • Caveat: two targets appear under retired names (pflege, hartmanndirect.com-de-de) whose series ended when the target was renamed, and HC/HD series are short, so their totals are not comparable with the rest.
